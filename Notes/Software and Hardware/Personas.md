> [!NOTE] Definition
> A persona is a fictional character created from user research to represent one type of user who might interact with a product, capturing their needs, experiences, behaviors, and goals.

## Why Personas Are Useful

- Help designers understand users' **needs, experience, behaviors, and goals** in a concrete, memorable form
- Help the design team **"step out of themselves"** - designers are typically not representative of the target user (e.g., they have a technical background and above-average digital literacy), and a persona counteracts the tendency to design for oneself
- Give the team a shared reference point during design discussions ("would Rebecca understand this menu?")

## Types of Personas

| Type | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Goal-directed Persona** | Centered on what the user wants to achieve |
| **Role-based Persona** | Centered on the user's role or job function |
| **Engaging Persona** | Written as a rich narrative to build empathy |
| **Fictional Persona** | A composite character not tied to a single real interviewee |

## Antipersona

An **antipersona** represents a user the product is explicitly **not** designed for, or a malicious actor the design must defend against (e.g., a "Hotel Burglar" antipersona used when designing hotel room security features). Antipersonas clarify design boundaries by making explicit who or what the system should exclude or resist, alongside the target users it should support.

## Personas and Scenarios

A persona is typically embedded inside a [[Scenarios in HCI|scenario]] - a persona describes *who* the user is, while a scenario describes a *story* of that persona trying to achieve a goal within a specific context.

## Example

"Rebecca, 26, a casual audiophile and frontend developer" is a persona used to design a music streaming feature. Her goals (discovering new artists, listening efficiently on limited mobile data), frustrations (the "filter bubble" effect), and habits (listening while working) directly shape which features are prioritized.
