> [!NOTE] Definition
> Ideation methods are structured techniques used to generate many candidate solutions to a design problem, deliberately favoring quantity and diversity of ideas over early judgment or refinement.

## Sketching

Rapid, low-commitment visual generation of ideas. The point is not artistic quality but speed - sketches are a way to think, not just to record a finished thought.

## Brainstorming and Crazy 8

Classic group brainstorming follows a few key rules: defer judgment, encourage wild ideas, build on others' ideas, stay focused on the topic, and go for quantity.

**Crazy 8** is a timed brainstorming exercise: fold a sheet of paper into 8 sections and sketch 8 distinct ideas in 8 minutes (one minute per idea). The tight time pressure forces participants past their first, most obvious ideas into more creative territory.

## Brainwriting

A silent, written variant of brainstorming: participants write down ideas individually, then pass their sheet to the next person, who reads the existing ideas and adds new ones building on them. This avoids the social dynamics of spoken brainstorming (e.g., dominant voices, production blocking) and lets quieter participants contribute equally.

## Worst Possible Idea

Participants are asked to deliberately generate the *worst* possible solution to the problem. This lowers the pressure to be "correct," often produces genuinely funny and creative output, and frequently reveals a good idea hiding in the inverse of a bad one.

## Ideation Hexagon

A structured prompt technique that pushes ideation in six different directions (e.g., varying by user group, technology, context, or constraint) to systematically broaden the solution space beyond the first ideas that come to mind, rather than relying purely on unstructured free association.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Ideation methods are about **elaboration**, not decision-making - the goal is to maximize the number and diversity of candidate ideas before any convergence or selection happens.
