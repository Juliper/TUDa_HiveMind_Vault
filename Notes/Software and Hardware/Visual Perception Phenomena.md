> [!NOTE] Definition
> The human visual system introduces systematic distortions when interpreting stimuli, leading to predictable perceptual phenomena.

## Peripheral Resolution

Detail perception decreases with distance from the center of the visual field. Objects far from the fovea appear less sharp.

## Mach Effect

At the boundary between two regions of different contrast, the visual system perceives the transition as exaggerated - the dark side appears darker and the light side appears lighter than they actually are.

## Brightness vs. Lightness

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Brightness** | Perceived amount of light from a self-luminous source |
| **Lightness** | Perceived amount of light from a reflecting surface |

## Simultaneous Contrast

Objects appear brighter in darker surroundings and darker in brighter surroundings. The background context shifts perceived color and brightness.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These phenomena are not bugs but features of the visual system - they enhance edge detection and contrast in natural scenes. Understanding them is critical for display design and image processing.

## Color Perception Phenomena

- **Crispening Effect**: perceived color difference between two stimuli is amplified by a similar background
- **Stevens Effect**: perceived contrast increases with luminance
- **Hunt Effect**: perceived colorfulness increases with luminance
