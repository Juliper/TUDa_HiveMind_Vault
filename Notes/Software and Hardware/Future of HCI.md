> [!NOTE] Definition
> The future of HCI is generally described as "post-WIMP": interaction paradigms that move beyond windows, icons, menus, and a single pointer toward more natural, embodied, and pervasive forms of interaction.

## Directions Beyond WIMP

| Paradigm | Description |
|---|---|
| **Touch and gesture** | Direct multi-touch input replaces the mouse pointer |
| **Voice and conversational UI** | Natural language replaces menu navigation |
| **Virtual and Augmented Reality** | 3D spatial interaction replaces 2D windows |
| **Tangible interfaces** | Physical objects act as interface controls |
| **Ubiquitous computing** | Computing embedded invisibly into everyday objects and environments |
| **Brain-computer interfaces** | Direct neural signal input, bypassing motor action entirely |

## Ubiquitous Computing

Mark Weiser's vision (1991) of ubiquitous computing describes technology that "weaves itself into the fabric of everyday life until it is indistinguishable from it" - computing that disappears into the environment rather than requiring a dedicated device and screen.

## Why WIMP Struggles Here

The [[WIMP Paradigm]] assumes a single 2D screen, a single pointer, and a desktop metaphor. These assumptions break down for:

- **VR/AR** - no fixed 2D surface, spatial 3D interaction needed
- **Multi-user large displays** - multiple simultaneous pointers and collaborators
- **Wearables and IoT** - minimal or no screen at all

## Example

A smart speaker (voice-only interface) has no windows, icons, menus, or pointer - it relies entirely on conversational interaction, representing a complete departure from the WIMP model while still needing to manage [[Mental Models]] and provide clear feedback (narrowing the [[Gulf of Execution and Evaluation]]) through sound alone.
