> [!NOTE] Definition
> A focus group is a qualitative research method in which a purposely selected group of participants is interviewed together, using group dynamics and discussion to explore a topic in depth.

## Properties

- **Qualitative method** - produces rich, descriptive data rather than statistics
- The group is often **not a representative sample**, but a purposely selected set of participants relevant to the topic
- The interviewer's role is primarily to **keep the group on topic** while letting discussion flow naturally

## When to Use

Focus groups work best **early in the design process**, especially for exploring complex topics where group dynamics can surface perspectives that individual interviews might miss.

## Pros and Cons

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Depth through open-ended questions | Difficult to keep participants on topic |
| Group setting can feel more natural, increasing validity for some topics | Social dynamics can distort results (shy participants stay quiet, others exaggerate to impress the group) |
| Less time-intensive than running multiple individual interviews | Harder to attribute specific opinions to individual participants |

## Focus Groups vs. Interviews

Unlike a one-on-one [[Interview Techniques in HCI|interview]], a focus group deliberately leverages social interaction between participants - disagreement, building on each other's ideas, and group norms - as a source of insight, at the cost of losing some individual-level detail and control over the conversation.
