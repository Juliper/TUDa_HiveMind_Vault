> [!NOTE] Definition
> Ethnography is a qualitative research method in which the researcher directly observes (and often participates in) users' natural environment and activities, aiming to understand real practices, workarounds, and social context that would not surface through interviews alone.

## Why Ethnography Is Used in HCI

Users often cannot fully articulate how they actually work - habits are automatic, workarounds feel unremarkable, and social/organizational factors are invisible to the people embedded in them. Ethnography surfaces this tacit knowledge by observing behavior directly rather than relying on self-report.

This complements [[Situated Action]] theory: since action is improvised in response to immediate circumstances, understanding it requires observing those circumstances directly, not just asking users to describe an idealized plan.

## Methods

- **Passive observation** - watching users work without intervening
- **Participant observation** - the researcher takes part in the activity being studied
- **Contextual inquiry** - a hybrid of observation and in-context interview, asking users to explain actions as they perform them

## Strengths and Limitations

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Reveals actual behavior, not reported behavior | Time-consuming and resource-intensive |
| Surfaces unspoken workarounds and social context | Small sample sizes limit statistical generalization |
| Uncovers requirements users would never think to mention | Presence of an observer can alter behavior (observer effect) |

## Example

Observing nurses on a hospital ward reveals informal paper-based tracking systems used alongside an official electronic health record - a workaround invisible to the system's designers and one the nurses themselves might not think to mention in an interview, since it feels like "just how things are done."
