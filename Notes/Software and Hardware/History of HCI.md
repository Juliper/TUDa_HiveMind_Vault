> [!NOTE] Definition
> The history of HCI traces how the interface between humans and computers evolved from purely mechanical, expert-only operation toward direct, visual, general-public interaction.

## Early Computing (Batch Era)

The earliest computers, such as the **Zuse Z3** (1941), had no concept of interactive use. Programs were entered mechanically or via punch cards and processed in batches with no feedback loop - the "user" was an operator feeding jobs to the machine, not someone interacting with it in real time.

**Batch processing** dominated computing into the 1960s: jobs were queued, run sequentially, and results returned later, often hours afterward. There was no notion of an interface designed for human perception or cognition.

## VIPs

- **Vannevar Bush (1945)** proposed the Memex, a conceptual device for associative information storage.
- **Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad (1963)** was the first program allowing direct manipulation of graphical objects with a light pen, introducing constraint-based drawing and object-oriented graphics.
- **Douglas Engelbart's "Mother of All Demos" (1968)** introduced the mouse, hypertext linking, video conferencing, and windows in a single influential demonstration.

## The GUI / WIMP Era

The [[WIMP Paradigm]] emerged from Xerox PARC research (Alto, 1973; Star, 1981) and was commercialized by Apple (Macintosh, 1984) and Microsoft (Windows, 1985), shifting computing from expert command-line operation to visual, discoverable interaction usable by non-specialists.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The shift from batch processing to WIMP interfaces represents a fundamental change in who computers were designed for - from trained operators to the general public.
