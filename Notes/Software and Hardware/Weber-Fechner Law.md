> [!NOTE] Definition
> The Weber-Fechner laws describe the mathematical relationship between physical stimulus intensity and perceived sensation strength.

## Variables

- $L$: perceived brightness (Hellempfindungsstarke)
- $R$: stimulus intensity (Reizintensitat)
- $k$: constant depending on sense modality (light, sound, pain, etc.)

## Weber's Law

The just-noticeable difference is a constant fraction of the stimulus:

$$\Delta L = \frac{\Delta R}{R} = \text{const.}$$

## Fechner's Law

Perceived intensity is proportional to the logarithm of the stimulus:

$$L = c_1 \cdot \log R$$

## Stevens' Power Law

A more general formulation using a power function:

$$E = c_2 \cdot R^k$$

For visual stimuli, $E = L$.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These laws explain why, for example, doubling the physical brightness of a light does not double the perceived brightness. Perception is logarithmic, not linear.
