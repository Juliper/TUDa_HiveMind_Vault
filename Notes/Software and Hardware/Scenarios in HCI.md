> [!NOTE] Definition
> A user scenario is a story that designers create to show how a user might act to achieve a goal in a system or environment, typically built around a specific [[Personas|persona]] and focused on the most common use cases.

## Structure of a Scenario

| Element | Question Answered |
|---|---|
| **Background** | Who are your users, including their knowledge base and skillset? |
| **Motivations** | What goals do they want to achieve? |
| **Tasks** | What must they do to reach those goals? |
| **Context of use** | How will they encounter your design? |
| **Environment** | Where will they try to use it? |
| **Challenges** | What can get in their way when they try to use it (e.g., signal loss)? |

## Why Scenarios Matter

Scenarios ground abstract user requirements in a concrete narrative, making it easier for a design team to reason about whether a proposed solution actually helps a specific user accomplish a specific goal under realistic conditions - including the obstacles that could get in the way.

## Example

A scenario for a music app persona: "Rebecca listens to music constantly while working from her MacBook and iPhone. She wants to discover new artists but is too busy to browse manually, and worries about her mobile data usage while streaming. One afternoon, at a cafe with weak WiFi, she tries to find a new artist recommendation without using up her data plan."

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A good scenario captures not just the happy path but the challenges a user faces - a scenario that never goes wrong is not useful for finding design gaps.

## Related Concepts

- [[Personas]]: a scenario is usually built around a specific persona
- [[Human-Centered Design Process]]: scenarios are produced during the "specify user requirements" stage and used to guide the "design solutions" stage
- [[Prototyping in HCI]]: scenarios are often used directly to scope horizontal vs. vertical prototypes
